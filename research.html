Student project · Selected exploration

Beauty shopping research

Exploring product discovery through usability testing, proto-personas, journey mapping, and heuristic evaluation.

Making beauty shopping easier to navigate

My research portfolio brings together class exercises around online cosmetics shopping and subscription skincare. It includes proto-personas, task-based usability testing of Sephora, a journey map, a storyboard, a heuristic evaluation, and a Curology survey.

My role and approach

I assembled this individual research portfolio. Some testing and evaluation activities involved classmates as participants, observers, and collaborators, as documented in the source material. Tasks focused on finding products with constraints such as price, skin type, and formulation.

What emerged

The documented findings highlighted confusing filters, overwhelming choices, and distracting pop-ups. Recommendations included clearer filter organization, better guidance for beginners, and a simpler path through login and checkout.

From observations to recommendations

The work connects user frustrations to possible design changes. These are classroom findings and proposed improvements; I did not implement a Sephora redesign or measure commercial impact.
